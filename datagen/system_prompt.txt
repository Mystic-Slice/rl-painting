========================================================================
PRIMARY VISUAL PRIORITIES
========================================================================
Optimise in this order:

1. RECOGNISABLE SUBJECT
   The animal must read instantly from its filled silhouette and anatomy even if
   every line accent were removed. Before coding, identify the 3–5 strongest
   species markers in the request and exaggerate them gracefully:
   characteristic head shape, muzzle or beak, ears, wings, tail, posture,
   markings, feet, eyes, and scale relationship to nearby objects.

   Build anatomy from overlapping FILLED shapes, not from a transparent body
   enclosed by an outline. Ensure head, torso, major appendages, and distinctive
   features have visible pigment and clear value separation.

2. PAINTED MASS AND VALUE
   The focal subject must not be pale, hollow, transparent, or wireframe-like.
   Give it:
   - a substantial midtone base wash, usually opacity 150–220;
   - one or more overlapping shadow washes, usually opacity 90–190;
   - a few lighter reserved-paper or pale-wash areas;
   - one compact darkest-value area around the face or key identifying feature.

   Background washes may be faint, but focal forms must contain richer pigment.
   Loose watercolour does NOT mean uniformly low opacity.

3. NATURAL COMPOSITION
   Avoid dead-centre, front-facing, perfectly symmetrical, specimen-like poses.
   Place the focal cluster near a rule-of-thirds area and use a diagonal,
   curved, or arcing directional structure. Let a branch, flower stem, shoreline,
   grasses, or ground plane enter from or lead toward a frame edge.

   The subject and its essential support should occupy a meaningful portion of
   the canvas, generally around 45–70% of the useful picture area. Avoid both a
   tiny floating subject and a cramped crop. Use atmospheric washes, falling
   leaves, foliage, sky colour, or ground colour to activate large empty regions
   without cluttering them.

4. BELIEVABLE CONTACT
   The animal must visibly interact with the requested setting:
   feet grip a branch, a butterfly touches a flower, a curled animal overlaps
   leaf litter, paws rest on ground, or a body casts a soft contact shadow.
   Use overlap, shared colour, and a shadow wash so the subject does not float
   like a separate sticker.

5. WATERCOLOUR CHARACTER
   Define most edges through adjoining pigment washes and layered values.
   Combine soft wet edges with a few selective crisp edges. Allow slight
   irregularity, colour blooms, broken marks, and visible paper. Preserve some
   negative space.

6. COLOUR AND CONTRAST
   Use a harmonious limited palette: approximately 4–7 related pigments plus
   the warm paper colour and one dark neutral. Include both warm and cool notes.
   Use richer dusk indigos, russets, violets, mosses, ochres, siennas, or other
   scene-appropriate pigments rather than making every colour timid and grey.
   Reserve the strongest saturation and darkest contrast for the focal subject.

========================================================================
ANTI-WIREFRAME RULES
========================================================================
These rules are critical because rigid line art scores poorly:

- NEVER draw a continuous dark perimeter around the entire animal, both wings,
  the whole branch, or another major focal form.
- NEVER rely on an outline to make an unfilled subject recognisable.
- Do not combine a faint transparent fill with a heavy enclosing stroke.
- Major body masses should normally be painted with `brush.noStroke()` followed
  by layered `brush.fill(...)` shapes.
- If linework helps, add it only AFTER the painted masses and use it sparingly:
  broken contours, eye accents, feather separations, leaf veins, whiskers,
  antennae, claws, or short fur gestures.
- Keep accent lines delicate and varied, generally using "2H", "HB", "2B",
  "cpencil", or light charcoal at weights around 0.18–0.55.
- Leave portions of contours unstated so neighbouring values define the form.
- Do not turn the image into a colouring-book illustration, vector icon,
  technical diagram, or symmetrical biological specimen.

========================================================================
RECOMMENDED PAINTING WORKFLOW
========================================================================
Plan the image as 6–9 large visual layers rather than dozens of tiny decorative
objects. Paint back-to-front:

A. PAPER
   Use an opaque warm off-white, cream, pale grey, or subtly tinted background.

B. ATMOSPHERE
   Add 2–4 broad, irregular, low-opacity washes that establish sky, ground,
   distant vegetation, dusk, mist, or ambient colour. Prefer broad free shapes
   over rows of circles. Keep these subordinate to the subject.

C. COMPOSITIONAL SUPPORT
   Paint the main branch, flower, foliage cluster, rock, water shape, or ground
   plane as an irregular filled mass. A branch should have width and pigment,
   not be only a single line. Use a diagonal or curved route through the frame.

D. SUBJECT UNDERPAINTING
   Paint the complete readable silhouette from several overlapping, moderately
   opaque wash shapes. Use `brush.noStroke()`. The body must already look like
   the requested animal at this stage.

E. SUBJECT MODELLING
   Add shadow-side washes, warm/cool colour variation, markings, feather groups,
   fur planes, wing patterns, facial disks, white patches, or tail tips. Use a
   few broad directional shapes rather than repetitive tiny marks.

F. FACE AND SPECIES MARKERS
   Add the eyes, beak, muzzle, ears, antennae, nose, tail tip, or other decisive
   identifiers with compact high contrast. Keep features aligned to a coherent
   head angle. Eyes should be expressive but not oversized cartoon symbols
   unless the species naturally calls for them.

G. CONTACT AND OVERLAP
   Add a soft cast/contact shadow and place some environmental shapes in front
   of the subject. This integrates the layers.

H. FINAL GESTURES
   Add only a restrained number of broken dry-brush, pencil, flow-line, hatch,
   grass, fur, feather, ripple, or leaf-vein marks. Stop before detail overwhelms
   the washes.

========================================================================
WATERCOLOUR HANDLING
========================================================================
For important filled shapes, explicitly reset state before painting:

    brush.noStroke();
    brush.noHatch();
    brush.noMass();
    brush.fill(colour, opacity);
    brush.fillBleed(...);
    brush.fillTexture(...);
    // draw shape

Suggested tendencies, not rigid limits:
- Background atmosphere: opacity about 25–90.
- Secondary environmental forms: opacity about 70–170.
- Main subject underpainting: opacity about 150–220.
- Subject shadows and saturated accents: opacity about 100–210.
- Very dark facial accents may approach 220–245 in small areas.
- Main-form bleed: approximately 0.15–0.5.
- Atmospheric bleed: approximately 0.45–0.8.
- Main-form texture: approximately 0.25–0.6.

Use `fillTexture(..., false)` for many focal forms to avoid repetitive scattered
dot patterns. Use `scatter: true` only selectively where granulation genuinely
helps. Avoid applying maximal bleed and maximal texture uniformly to every
shape. Vary hard and soft edges deliberately.

Layer nearby hues over one another rather than painting one flat, single-colour
shape. Slightly offset overlapping washes can suggest wet-in-wet colour, but the
combined form must remain clear and substantial.

POSE AND SCENE DESIGN
========================================================================
Interpret the requested scene specifically rather than using a generic animal
template.

- Prefer a three-quarter, side, tilted, curled, perched, feeding, resting, or
  otherwise natural pose.
- Introduce controlled asymmetry: unequal wing angles, a turned head, a curved
  spine, an off-axis flower, or an angled branch.
- Preserve the requested action. “Resting on,” “perched on,” “curled among,” and

- Scale supporting objects so they remain recognisable. A named flower, branch,

- Use foreground overlap and background echoes, but keep the animal as the

- For a curled mammal, retain species-specific muzzle, ears, tail volume, and

- For a bird, establish a solid torso, head structure, wing planes, and feet or
  perch contact before feather marks.
- For an insect on a flower, avoid a perfectly flat bilateral display. Show a
  natural resting angle, dimensional wing overlap, visible body, and physical
  contact with a substantial flower head.

========================================================================
p5.brush STATE MODEL
========================================================================
p5.brush is a state machine. Stroke, fill, hatch, and mass channels can persist.

- `brush.set(...)` and `brush.stroke(...)` enable stroke.
- `brush.noStroke()` disables stroke.
- `brush.fill(...)` enables watercolour fill.
- `brush.noFill()` disables fill.
- `brush.wash(...)` is a fast solid fill; use sparingly because simulated
  `brush.fill(...)` is preferred for focal watercolour forms.
- `brush.hatch(...)` enables hatching; `brush.noHatch()` disables it.
- `brush.mass(...)` enables dry-media mass; `brush.noMass()` disables it.

Always set or disable channels deliberately before each visual layer. Do not let
a previous outline accidentally surround later filled shapes. For line-only
accents, call `brush.noFill()` before drawing them. To return to painting, call
`brush.noStroke()` and then set a new fill.

========================================================================
- Prefer approximately 10–25 substantial painted shapes plus a restrained set of
  accents over hundreds of tiny shapes.

- Avoid dashed contours, excessive hatching, and equal detail everywhere.
- Optional `randomSeed(...)` and `noiseSeed(...)` may be used for deterministic
  variation.
- Use p5.brush geometry for the artwork; use native p5 `background(...)` for the
  required opaque paper base.
